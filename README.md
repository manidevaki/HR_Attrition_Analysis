HR Employee Attrition Analytics

An end-to-end HR Employee Attrition Analytics project that uses Excel, SQL, and Power BI to identify employee attrition patterns and translate them into actionable HR insights.

📌 Project Overview

Employee attrition is a major HR and business challenge because frequent employee exits can increase recruitment costs, reduce productivity, and affect team stability.

This project analyzes employee-level HR data to answer key business questions such as:

What is the overall employee attrition rate?

Which departments and job roles have the highest attrition?

Does overtime relate to employee attrition?

How do age, job level, satisfaction, travel, and tenure relate to attrition?

Which employee segments may represent higher attrition risk?

The analysis combines data exploration, SQL business analysis, and Power BI visualization.

🎯 Business Objective

The primary objective is to identify the factors and employee segments associated with higher attrition so that HR teams can:

Identify high-risk employee groups

Understand major attrition drivers

Prioritize retention initiatives

Improve employee experience

Support data-driven workforce decisions

📈 Key Findings:

The analysis reveals several useful patterns:

1. Overall Attrition

The dataset contains 1,470 employees, of whom 237 left the organization, resulting in an overall attrition rate of 16.12%.

2. Department-Level Differences

Attrition varies across departments, with Sales showing the highest attrition rate among the departments in this dataset (20.63%).

3. Job Role Differences

Attrition also differs substantially by job role. Sales Representative has the highest observed attrition rate (39.76%).

4. Overtime

Employees working overtime have an attrition rate of 30.53%, compared with 10.44% among employees without overtime.

This makes overtime an important factor to investigate when designing retention strategies.

5. Age

Attrition varies by age group, with Under 25 having the highest observed rate (39.18%).

6. Early Tenure

Employees with 0 years at the company show an attrition rate of 36.36%, highlighting the importance of examining onboarding and early-career employee experience.

💡 Business Recommendations

Based on the observed patterns, HR teams could consider:

Strengthening onboarding and early-tenure support

Reviewing overtime workload and workload distribution

Investigating retention challenges within high-attrition departments and roles

Using employee satisfaction metrics as an early warning signal

Reviewing career progression and promotion opportunities

Creating targeted retention programs instead of applying one strategy to every employee group

These recommendations should be validated with additional organizational data before being used for policy decisions.
